Motiva X1

Beyond Limits, Into Motion.

A camera-based eye-control wheelchair concept designed to translate a user's eye movements into movement commands for a wheelchair.

See How It Works → Our Mission
THE PROBLEM

When controlling movement isn't easy.

People with severe physical disabilities may have difficulty operating conventional wheelchair controls. Motiva X1 explores a different way of interacting with a wheelchair: using intentional eye movements as an input.

♿

Mobility Challenges

Some people may have very limited control over their arms and hands, making conventional controls difficult to operate.

🖐️

Traditional Controls

A joystick or physical controller may not be suitable for every wheelchair user.

👁️

A New Interface

Motiva X1 investigates the possibility of using eye movements as a natural method of giving movement commands.

THE SOLUTION

Your eyes become the interface.

Motiva X1 uses a smartphone camera mounted on the wheelchair. The user places the phone into a holder and opens the Motiva X1 application. The camera observes the user's face and eyes, while the application analyzes the eye movement and determines the intended direction. The resulting command is then sent to the wheelchair's control system.

HOW IT WORKS

From eye movement to motion.

Motiva X1 connects a smartphone camera, software and an Arduino-based motor control system into one concept.

01 • POSITION

Phone Holder

The smartphone is placed securely into a holder positioned so that the camera can observe the user.

02 • CAMERA

Eye Detection

The phone's camera captures the user's face and eye movements.

03 • APP

Motiva X1 App

The application analyzes the camera information and determines the intended movement.

04 • COMMAND

Send Direction

The detected eye movement is converted into a movement command for the controller.

05 • ARDUINO

Process

The Arduino receives the command and controls the wheelchair's motor system.

06 • MOTION

Move

The motors respond to the command, allowing the wheelchair to move according to the detected input.

THE SMARTPHONE

The phone is the eyes of Motiva X1.

Instead of requiring a dedicated camera system, Motiva X1 uses a smartphone placed into a holder attached to the wheelchair.

The phone runs the Motiva X1 app. Its camera provides the visual input required to interpret the user's eye movements.

This approach is designed around hardware that is already widely available: a smartphone.

Motiva X1 is more than a wheelchair control system.

It represents our vision of using accessible technology to create greater independence and freedom of movement for people who may not be able to use conventional controls.

By combining a smartphone camera, intelligent software, Arduino and motor control, Motiva X1 explores a new way for people to interact with mobility technology.
